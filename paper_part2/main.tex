\begin{abstract}
Let $R(k_1,\dots,k_r;t)$ denote the smallest integer $n$ such that every
$r$-colouring of the $t$-element subsets of an $n$-element set contains, for
some colour $c\in\{1,\dots,r\}$, a monochromatic $k_c$-element subset in
colour~$c$, and write $R_r(k;t)$ when $k_1=\dots=k_r=k$. Using three families of
group-invariant constructions---multi-infinity cyclic relaxations on
$\Z_m\cup\{\infty_0,\dots,\infty_{s-1}\}$, infinity-free $2$-homogeneous and
odd-order affine actions on $\F_p$, and narrow or chiral-split
$\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)$ actions on $\F_q\cup\{\infty\}$ with $q\equiv 2,3\pmod 5$---paired
with GPU stochastic local search and counterexample-guided SAT refinement, we
construct sixteen explicit hypergraph colourings establishing:
\begin{align*}
  &R(5,5;3)\ge 110,\quad R(6,6;3)\ge 308,\quad R(5,7;3)\ge 284,\\
  &R(5,6;3)\ge 156,\quad R(4,8;3)\ge 164,\quad R(4,7;3)\ge 108,\\
  &R(4,4,4;3)\ge 103,\quad R(4,4,5;3)\ge 164,\quad R(4,5,5;3)\ge 272,\\
  &R_4(4;3)\ge 381,\quad R_5(4;3)\ge 1260,\quad R_6(4;3)\ge 3780,\quad
   R_7(4;3)\ge 5232,\\
  &R_3(5;4)\ge 969,\quad R_4(5;4)\ge 3805,\quad R_5(5;4)\ge 9439.
\end{align*}
In particular, $R(5,5;3)\ge 110$ improves the bound $88$ in Revision~\#18 of
Radziszowski's dynamic survey (and the recent 2026 bounds $99$ and $101$ of
Lysenst{\o}en), while $R(4,4,4;3)\ge 103$ improves $79$ in the survey, $84$
(Yang, 2026), and $100$ in the author's preceding note. Via the lexicographic
product theorem of Bruce, Budden, and Hiller and known stepping-up lemmas, these
constructions further imply $R(5,5,5;3)\ge 11\,882$,
$R_4(5;3)\ge 141\,158\,162$, $R_r(5;3)\ge 109^{2^{r-2}}+1$ for all $r\ge 3$,
$R(11,11;4)>2^{307}$, $R_6(5;4)>2^{102}$, $R_{10}(5;4)>2^{5231}$, and
$R_8(6;5)>2^{9438}$. All sixteen witnesses are archived in a uniform
machine-readable format and verified by standalone zero-dependency Rust and
Python clean-room programs in $2.5$ seconds.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For integers $t\ge 2$ and $k_1,\dots,k_r>t$, the \emph{hypergraph Ramsey number}
$R(k_1,\dots,k_r;t)$ is the least integer $n$ such that every colouring
$\chi\colon\binom{X}{t}\to\{0,\dots,r-1\}$ of the $t$-subsets of an $n$-element
set $X$ admits an index $c\in\{0,\dots,r-1\}$ and a $k_{c+1}$-subset
$S\subseteq X$ with $\chi(e)=c$ for all $e\in\binom{S}{t}$. When
$k_1=\dots=k_r=k$, we write $R_r(k;t)$. A colouring of $\binom{X}{t}$ on
$|X|=n$ vertices free of such monochromatic subsets will be called a
\emph{$(k_1,\dots,k_r;t)$-colouring of order~$n$}, certifying
$R(k_1,\dots,k_r;t)\ge n+1$.

Apart from $R(4,4;3)=13$~\cite{mr1} (with $R(4,5;3)\ge 35$~\cite{dyb2}), exact
values of classical hypergraph Ramsey numbers remain out of reach, and
constructive lower bounds rely on carefully chosen symmetry groups balancing
orbit compression against forced monochromatic cliques. Revision~\#18 of
Radziszowski's dynamic survey~\cite[Section~7.1]{survey} lists $R(5,5;3)\ge 88$
and $R(4,4,4;3)\ge 79$ due to Dybizba\'nski~\cite{dyb3}, together with
$R(5,5,5;3)\ge 7570$ via the lexicographic product of Bruce, Budden, and
Hiller~\cite{brbh}, and $R_4(5;3)\ge 131\,073$ from Budden, Hiller, and
Rapp~\cite{budhr1}. Very recently, Yang~\cite{yang} obtained $R(4,4,4;3)\ge 84$;
Lysenst{\o}en~\cite{lysenstoen1,lysenstoen2} established $R(5,5;3)\ge 99$ and
subsequently $R(5,5;3)\ge 101$ (together with $R(5,7;4)\ge 859$ and
Lean-verified improvements to the stepping-up lemma); and in a companion
note~\cite{hashimoto1} the author constructed explicit witnesses for
$R(4,4,4;3)\ge 100$, $R(5,7;4)\ge 711$, and $R(6,6;4)\ge 685$.

Meanwhile, many natural adjacent parameters---including the third diagonal
$3$-uniform number $R(6,6;3)$, off-diagonal $3$-uniform numbers $R(4,7;3)$,
$R(4,8;3)$, $R(5,6;3)$, $R(5,7;3)$, mixed $3$-colour numbers $R(4,4,5;3)$ and
$R(4,5,5;3)$, the multicolour tetrahedral numbers $R_r(4;3)$ for $r\ge 4$ (where
quadratic blow-up constructions fail because any $2$-blowup of an edge creates
four vertices spanning monochromatic triples), and the multicolour $4$-uniform
numbers $R_r(5;4)$ for $r\ge 3$---have had no explicit group-invariant
constructions recorded in~\cite{survey}.

In this paper we address both regimes systematically and establish sixteen
explicit lower bounds (convention: four-digit integers are written without
separators, while integers of five or more digits use thin spaces):

\begin{theorem}[Two-colour $3$-uniform bounds]\label{thm:2col3u}
\begin{align*}
  R(5,5;3)&\ge 110, & R(6,6;3)&\ge 308, & R(5,7;3)&\ge 284,\\
  R(5,6;3)&\ge 156, & R(4,8;3)&\ge 164, & R(4,7;3)&\ge 108.
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Multicolour $3$-uniform bounds]\label{thm:mcol3u}
\[
  R(4,4,4;3)\ge 103,\qquad R(4,4,5;3)\ge 164,\qquad R(4,5,5;3)\ge 272,
\]
and for the $r$-colour tetrahedral Ramsey numbers $R_r(4;3)$ ($4\le r\le 7$),
\[
  R_4(4;3)\ge 381,\qquad R_5(4;3)\ge 1260,\qquad R_6(4;3)\ge 3780,\qquad
  R_7(4;3)\ge 5232.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Multicolour $4$-uniform bounds]\label{thm:mcol4u}
\[
  R_3(5;4)\ge 969,\qquad R_4(5;4)\ge 3805,\qquad R_5(5;4)\ge 9439.
\]
\end{theorem}

Combining Theorems~\ref{thm:2col3u}--\ref{thm:mcol4u} with the product theorem
of Bruce, Budden, and Hiller~\cite{brbh} and the stepping-up lemmas of
Erd\H{o}s--Hajnal~\cite{grs} and Jartoux, Keller, Smorodinsky, and
Yuditsky~\cite{jaksy} yields the following consequences (proved in
Section~\ref{sec:conseq}):

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:conseq}
$R(5,5,5;3)\ge 11\,882$, $R_4(5;3)\ge 141\,158\,162$, and in general
$R_r(5;3)\ge 109^{2^{r-2}}+1$ for every $r\ge 3$. Furthermore,
$R(9,9;4)>2^{109}$, $R(11,11;4)>2^{307}$, $R_6(5;4)>2^{102}$,
$R_7(5;4)>2^{380}$, $R_8(5;4)>2^{1259}$, $R_9(5;4)>2^{3779}$,
$R_{10}(5;4)>2^{5231}$, $R_6(6;5)>2^{968}$, $R_7(6;5)>2^{3804}$, and
$R_8(6;5)>2^{9438}$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Multi-Infinity Cyclic Relaxations for \texorpdfstring{$R(5,5;3)$ and $R(4,4,4;3)$}{R(5,5;3) and R(4,4,4;3)}}\label{sec:multiinf}

We first state the uniform orbit indexing convention used for all thirteen
$3$-uniform witnesses in this work. Let $m\ge 1$, $s\ge 0$, and $n=m+s$, with
point set
\[
  X_{m,s}=\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}\cup\{\infty_0,\dots,\infty_{s-1}\},
\]
where $\infty_j$ is encoded by the integer $m+j$ under the standard linear order
$0<1<\dots<m+s-1$. For $\gcd(a,m)=1$ of multiplicative order $d=\mathrm{ord}_m(a)$,
the affine subgroup
\[
  G(m,a)=\{\,x\mapsto a^j x+b\bmod m \;:\; 0\le j<d,\; b\in\Z_m\,\},
\]
extended to fix each $\infty_j$ pointwise ($g(\infty_j)=\infty_j$), acts on
$\binom{X_{m,s}}{3}$. Every orbit $O\subseteq\binom{X_{m,s}}{3}$ has a unique
lexicographically smallest triple $(u,v,w)$ ($u<v<w$); we number all orbits
$O_0,O_1,\dots,O_{M-1}$ in strictly increasing lexicographic order of these
minimum representatives. Thus an $r$-colouring invariant under $G(m,a)$ is
uniquely specified by an $M$-character string over $\{0,\dots,r-1\}$.

\subsection{Climbing to \texorpdfstring{$R(5,5;3)\ge 110$ and $R(4,4,4;3)\ge 103$}{R(5,5;3) >= 110 and R(4,4,4;3) >= 103}}

Standard affine constructions adjoin at most a single fixed point $\infty$ to
$\Z_m$, because adding two fixed points $\infty_0,\infty_1$ under a nontrivial
multiplier subgroup of even order tends to create short orbits whose $5$-subsets
degrade satisfiability. However, when the multiplier subgroup is relaxed all the
way to the pure translation group $G(m,1)\cong\Z_m$ ($a=1$), adjoining
$s\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ distinguishable fixed points $\infty_0,\dots,\infty_{s-1}$
creates $\binom{m}{2}\cdot s/m\approx ms/2$ mixed orbits of type $\{x,y,\infty_j\}$,
$s(s-1)/2$ orbits of type $\{x,\infty_i,\infty_j\}$, and $\binom{s}{3}$
singleton orbits $\{\infty_i,\infty_j,\infty_\ell\}$ with zero forced
monochromatic $4$- or $5$-cliques.

Crucially, any valid colouring on $\Z_m\cup\{\infty_0,\dots,\infty_{s-1}\}$
provides a zero-violation warm start on all triples not involving $\infty_s$
when lifting to $\Z_m\cup\{\infty_0,\dots,\infty_s\}$. Executing this
warm-started relaxation chain via GPU weighted WalkSAT/G2WSAT polished with
large-neighbourhood CDCL SAT (\texttt{CaDiCaL}~\cite{cadical,pysat}) yielded two
chains of witnesses:
\begin{itemize}
\item For $R(5,5;3)$: starting from $G(106,23)$ ($d=4$, $n=106$, $468$ orbits),
  passing to pure $\Z_{106}$ translations and successively adjoining
  $\infty_0,\infty_1,\infty_2$ produced valid $(5,5;3)$-colourings at
  $n=107$ ($1873$ orbits), $n=108$ ($1927$ orbits), and $n=109$
  ($X_{106,3}$, $1983$ orbits, file \texttt{R553\_109\_points\_rule.json}),
  proving $R(5,5;3)\ge 110$.
\item For $R(4,4,4;3)$: starting from $T_{99}$ on $G(98,67)$ with one fixed
  point~\cite{hashimoto1}, relaxing to $\Z_{98}$ translations and adjoining
  $\infty_1,\infty_2,\infty_3$ produced valid $(4,4,4;3)$-colourings at
  $n=100$ ($1651$ orbits), $n=101$ ($1703$ orbits), and $n=102$
  ($X_{98,4}$, $1758$ orbits, file \texttt{R4443\_102\_points\_rule.json}),
  proving $R(4,4,4;3)\ge 103$. Notice that at $s=4$, the four fixed points span
  four singleton orbits $\{\infty_0,\infty_1,\infty_2\}$,
  $\{\infty_0,\infty_1,\infty_3\}$, $\{\infty_0,\infty_2,\infty_3\}$, and
  $\{\infty_1,\infty_2,\infty_3\}$, which receive non-monochromatic colours
  automatically.
\end{itemize}

\section{Infinity-Free \texorpdfstring{$2$}{2}-Homogeneous and Odd-Order Affine Colourings}\label{sec:affine}

\subsection{Eliminating Infinity-Point Colour Collapse in \texorpdfstring{$R_r(4;3)$}{R\_r(4;3)}}

A natural question when scaling $3$-uniform constructions to $r\ge 4$ colours is
why adjoining a fixed point $\infty$ to the index-$2$ affine group
$\mathrm{AGL}^+(1,p)=\{x\mapsto sx+b\bmod p : s\in\mathrm{QR}_p\}$ ($p\equiv 3\pmod 4$)
stalls abruptly near $n\approx 100$ for $R_4(4;3)$. The reason is group-theoretic:

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:collapse}
Let $p\equiv 3\pmod 4$ be prime. Then $\mathrm{AGL}^+(1,p)$ acts sharply
$2$-homogeneously on $\F_p$. Consequently, on $\F_p\cup\{\infty\}$, all
$\binom{p}{2}$ triples of the form $\{x,y,\infty\}$ ($x,y\in\F_p$) lie in a
single $\mathrm{AGL}^+(1,p)$-orbit $O_\infty$. In any $\mathrm{AGL}^+(1,p)$-invariant
$(4,\dots,4;3)$-colouring of $\F_p\cup\{\infty\}$ with $r$ colours, if $O_\infty$
has colour~$c$, then $\F_p$ cannot contain a single finite triple of colour~$c$;
thus the finite restriction collapses to an $(r-1)$-colouring of $\binom{\F_p}{3}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $p\equiv 3\pmod 4$, $-1\notin\mathrm{QR}_p$, so for any distinct
$x,y\in\F_p$ exactly one of $y-x$ and $x-y$ belongs to $\mathrm{QR}_p$. If
$y-x\in\mathrm{QR}_p$, the unique map $t\mapsto(y-x)^{-1}(t-x)$ in
$\mathrm{AGL}^+(1,p)$ sends $\{x,y\}$ to $\{0,1\}$. Hence $\{x,y,\infty\}$ maps
to $\{0,1,\infty\}$ for all $\{x,y\}\in\binom{\F_p}{2}$. If $\{u,v,w\}\subset\F_p$
also had colour~$c$, then all four triples of $\{u,v,w,\infty\}$ would have
colour~$c$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse} points immediately to two non-degenerate regimes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Pure infinity-free $\mathrm{AGL}^+(1,p)$ ($s=0$, $p\equiv 3\pmod 4$).}
  With no $\infty$ point, all $r$ colours remain fully active on $\F_p$, and
  sharp $2$-homogeneity collapses $\binom{p}{3}$ into $(p-1)/3$ or $(p-2)/3$
  orbits ($\lfloor(p-1)/3\rfloor$ orbits) evaluated in $O(1)$ time: for
  $0\le a<b<c<p$, let $\Delta=b-a$, and set
  \[
    z=\begin{cases}
      (c-a)\Delta^{-1}\bmod p & \text{if }\Delta\in\mathrm{QR}_p,\\
      (c-b)(p-\Delta)^{-1}\bmod p & \text{if }\Delta\notin\mathrm{QR}_p,
    \end{cases}
  \]
  which maps $\{a,b,c\}$ to $\{0,1,z\}$ ($z\in\{2,\dots,p-1\}$). Each orbit of
  $\{0,1,z\}$ has size $1$ or $3$ (namely $\{z,z^{-1},-(z-1)^{-1}\}$ etc.\
  according to quadratic character), and ordering orbits by their smallest
  $z_{\min}\in\{2,\dots,p-1\}$ coincides identically with our lexicographic
  minimum-triple rule $\{0,1,z_{\min}\}$. Moreover, every $4$-subset of $\F_p$
  maps into $\{0,1,z,w\}$ ($2\le z<w<p$), reducing exhaustive verification on
  $p=5231$ vertices ($\binom{5231}{4}\approx 3.11\times 10^{13}$ $4$-subsets)
  to just $\binom{5229}{2}=13\,668\,606$ pairs $(z,w)$.
\item \emph{Odd-order subgroups $G(p,a)$ with $d=\mathrm{ord}_p(a)$ odd.}
  Because $-1$ has even order~$2$, $-1\notin\langle a\rangle$ whenever $d$ is
  odd, preventing self-paired $4$-subset degeneracies for every prime~$p$
  (including $p\equiv 1\pmod 4$). When the index $(p-1)/d\ge 4$, even adjoining
  $+\infty$ splits $\{x,y,\infty\}$ into $(p-1)/(2d)\ge 2$ distinct orbits,
  breaking Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse}. At $p=379$ with $a=255$ ($d=21$, index~$18$,
  $1\infty$), this yields an $1152$-orbit $4$-colouring on $380$ vertices
  (\texttt{R44443\_380\_points\_rule.json}), pushing $R_4(4;3)\ge 381$ well past the
  pure $\mathrm{AGL}^+(1,p)$ threshold.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Projective-Line \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)$}{PSL(2,q)} Constructions for \texorpdfstring{$R_r(5;4)$}{R\_r(5;4)}}\label{sec:psl4}

Let $q$ be an odd prime and $L_q=\F_q\cup\{\infty\}$ ($|L_q|=q+1$, with $\infty$
encoded as~$q$). The group $\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)$ acts on $L_q$ by M\"obius
transformations $x\mapsto(ax+b)/(cx+d)$ with $ad-bc\in\mathrm{QR}_q$. Because
$|\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)|=q(q^2-1)/2$, whenever $q\equiv 2$ or $3\pmod 5$ we have
$5\nmid q(q^2-1)/2$, so $\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)$ contains no element of order~$5$;
consequently no $5$-subset of $L_q$ can have all five of its $4$-subsets collapsed
into a single $\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)$-orbit.

For any $4$-subset $\{a,b,c,d\}\subset L_q$ ordered as $0\le a<b<c<d\le q$, put
$\Delta=(b-a)(c-a)(c-b)\bmod q$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Case 1 ($q\equiv 3\pmod 4$: $q=967$ and $q=3803$).} Let
  $\lambda=(a-c)(b-c)^{-1}\bmod q$ if $d=q$, and
  $\lambda=(a-c)(b-d)((a-d)(b-c))^{-1}\bmod q$ if $d<q$. As in~\cite{lysenstoen1,hashimoto1},
  $\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)$ partitions $\F_q\setminus\{0,1\}$ into narrow cross-ratio
  classes: $\{\lambda,(1-\lambda)^{-1},(\lambda-1)\lambda^{-1}\}$ of size~$3$ when
  $-\lambda\in\mathrm{QR}_q$ and $\lambda-1\in\mathrm{QR}_q$, and the full
  $6$-element $S_4$-orbit otherwise. Numbering these classes $C_0,\dots,C_{M-1}$
  by smallest element in $\{2,\dots,q-1\}$, the orbit of $\{a,b,c,d\}$ is the
  class of $\lambda$ if $\Delta\in\mathrm{QR}_q$, and the class of
  $\lambda^{-1}\bmod q$ if $\Delta\notin\mathrm{QR}_q$.
\item \textbf{Case 2 ($q\equiv 1\pmod 4$: $q=9437$).} Let
  $\kappa=(c-a)(c-b)^{-1}\bmod q$, and $\lambda=\kappa$ if $d=q$ else
  $\kappa(d-b)(d-a)^{-1}\bmod q$. Each $6$-element $\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)$
  cross-ratio class $P$ (ordered by smallest representative $x_{\min}\in\{2,\dots,q-1\}$)
  splits into two chiral $\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)$-orbits $(P^+,P^-)$ selected by
  whether $\Delta\in\mathrm{QR}_q$ if and only if both $x_{\min}\in\mathrm{QR}_q$
  and $x_{\min}-1\in\mathrm{QR}_q$, and remains a single orbit otherwise~\cite{hashimoto1}.
\end{itemize}

For $r=3$ colours at $q=967$ ($967\equiv 3\pmod 4$, $967\equiv 2\pmod 5$, $203$
narrow classes), incremental CDCL SAT solving with \texttt{CaDiCaL} produced an
explicit $(5,5,5;4)$-colouring on $968$ points (\texttt{R5554\_968\_points\_rule.json}),
whose $203$-character narrow-class string ($52+52+52+47$ characters) is printed in

\section{Consequences and Clean-Room Verification}\label{sec:conseq}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:conseq}]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:2col3u}, $R(5,5;3)\ge 110$, so there exists a $(5,5;3)$-colouring
of order $109$. Applying the iterated lexicographic product construction of
Bruce, Budden, and Hiller~\cite{brbh} (see also~\cite[Section~7.1(a)]{survey}),
if $R(5,5;3)>m$ then $R_r(5;3)\ge m^{2^{r-2}}+1$ for every $r\ge 3$. Setting
$m=109$ gives $R_3(5;3)\ge 109^2+1=11\,882$ and
$R_4(5;3)\ge 109^4+1=141\,158\,162$.

Next, the Erd\H{o}s--Hajnal stepping-up lemma~\cite[p.~106]{grs}
(\cite[Section~7.5(a)]{survey}) states that if $R(k,k;t)>m$ for $k>t\ge 3$, then
$R(2k+t-4,2k+t-4;t+1)>2^m$. Applying this with $(k,t,m)=(5,3,109)$ and
$(6,3,307)$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:2col3u} yields $R(9,9;4)>2^{109}$ and
$R(11,11;4)>2^{307}$. Finally, Jartoux, Keller, Smorodinsky, and
Yuditsky~\cite{jaksy} (\cite[Section~7.5(b)]{survey}) proved that if
$R_r(k;t)>m$ for $t\ge 3$, then $R_{r+3}(k+1;t+1)>2^m$. Substituting
$R_r(4;3)>102,380,1259,3779,5231$ ($r=3,\dots,7$) from
Theorem~\ref{thm:mcol3u} and $R_r(5;4)>968,3804,9438$ ($r=3,4,5$) from
Theorem~\ref{thm:mcol4u} gives all remaining inequalities.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Independent Clean-Room Verification}

To preclude any shared-code defect or convention mismatch between the GPU/SAT
search programs and verification, we wrote two standalone, zero-dependency
clean-room verifiers (\texttt{verify\_\allowbreak part2\_\allowbreak records.rs}
in pure Rust standard library and
\texttt{verify\_\allowbreak part2\_\allowbreak records.py} in pure Python
standard library) directly from the mathematical definitions in
Sections~\ref{sec:multiinf}--\ref{sec:psl4}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Cryptographic integrity and generator invariance.} Both verifiers
  recompute the FIPS~180-4 SHA-256 digest of every \texttt{orbit\_string} (listed
  in Table~\ref{tab:digests}) and explicitly check invariance under the group
  generators ($x\mapsto x+1$, $x\mapsto ax$, and $x\mapsto -x^{-1}$ where
  applicable).
\item \emph{Exhaustive forbidden-clique absence ($2.47\text{ s}$ total in Rust).}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item For $R(5,5;3)\ge 110$, $R(4,4,4;3)\ge 103$, and $R(4,4,5;3)\ge 164$,
    \texttt{verify\_\allowbreak part2\_\allowbreak records.rs} checks \emph{every}
    vertex anchor $0\le v_0<n$ with bitmask clique backtracking, assuming zero
    group symmetry whatsoever.
  \item For the remaining $G(m,a)+s\infty$ witnesses ($n\le 380$), verified
    $\Z_m$-translation invariance maps every subset containing a finite vertex to
    one containing~$0$; \texttt{verify\_\allowbreak part2\_\allowbreak records.rs}
    exhaustively checks anchor~$0$ and all infinity anchors (assuming zero
    multiplicative symmetry).
  \item For the large $\mathrm{AGL}^+(1,p)$ witnesses ($p\in\{1259,3779,5231\}$),
    verified $2$-homogeneity maps every $4$-subset to $\{0,1,z,w\}$
    ($2\le z<w<p$), and all $789\,396$, $7\,130\,976$, and $13\,668\,606$ pairs
    $(z,w)$ are checked exhaustively.
  \item For the $\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)$ $4$-uniform witnesses
    ($q\in\{967,3803,9437\}$), $\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)$ is $2$-transitive on $L_q$
    and partitions triples $\{\infty,0,b\}$ into two orbits represented by
    $b\in\{1,g_{\mathrm{ns}}\}$ ($g_{\mathrm{ns}}\notin\mathrm{QR}_q$); every
    $5$-subset of $L_q$ therefore maps to $\{\infty,0,b,c,d\}$ with
    $b\in\{1,g_{\mathrm{ns}}\}$ and $1\le c<d<q$ ($c,d\ne b$), and all
    $930\,260$, $14\,443\,800$, and $89\,009\,790$ normalized $5$-subsets are
    verified exhaustively.
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
